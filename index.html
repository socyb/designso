Argument 01 · practice

Where we speak from: design as our shared practice

  • For years we have collaborated on projects crossing design practice and sociocybernetic theory — every project has confirmed how fertile this crossing is.
  • Live example: we designed the website of this conference → socyb.github.io/oax27
  • The programme you consulted to arrive at this session is a design act inside the system it describes: deciding what counts as “programme”, “venue”, “session”, “participant” draws the distinctions that make the conference observable.

Design is not outside sociocybernetics — it is one of its operations.

“To design is to decide what to see and what to ignore.” Manifesto of Sociocybernetic Design, §1 (Ortega & Cardiel, 2025) · orig. “Diseñar es decidir qué ver y qué ignorar.”

Argument 02 · question

From epistemology to ontology: a question inherited from Kraków

  • At the 18th International Conference (Kraków 2024 — theme: Artificial Intelligence), ontology and epistemology in sociocybernetics were revisited through Kant’s questions — above all: What can I know?
“What can I know? What ought I to do? What may I hope?” Kant, Critique of Pure Reason, A805/B833 (1781/1998)
  • Our move: turn that question toward design.

What exists when we design?

What does design bring into existence — problems, users, needs, futures?

Sociocybernetics is uniquely equipped for an ontology of design: it treats observation, distinction, and system/environment as constitutive operations — not neutral descriptions.

Argument 03 · problem

The problem: a pre-given world

Design is usually treated as the production of objects, services, solutions. That frame silently assumes:

  • The designer is reified as an external controller.
  • The user is treated as a stable empirical object.
  • Problems are naturalized, as if they existed before framing.
  • Inputs are imagined as pure transfers of meaning.
  • Solutions are expected to close complex situations.

the world, delivered as a sealed package —
already cracking

A sociocybernetic ontology begins by making these assumptions observable.

What counts as a problem and what counts as its solution are functionally co-constructed, not given (Nassehi, 2019).

Argument 04 · tradition

We are not starting from zero: Pask, Glanville & design cybernetics

  • Gordon Pask — Conversation Theory; machines built for conversation (Musicolour, Colloquy of Mobiles). Already in 1969: the responsible architect cannot stand outside and watch the evolution of the structure as something that merely happens to it (Pask, 1969, p. 495).
  • Ranulph Glanville — extended conversation to all designing. Conversation generates novelty, not merely agreement: no meaning is transmitted — each participant constructs their own understanding.
“Cybernetics is the theory of design and design is the action of cybernetics.” Glanville (2007/2019, p. 25)
Pask 1969 · architecture as conversation Glanville 2007 · design ⇄ cybernetics Fischer & Herr 2019 · design cybernetics Ortega & Cardiel 2026 · sociocybernetic extension

Try again. Fail again. Fail better — design as an iterative conversation with failure (Glanville, 2007/2019).

The lens shifts attention from “what is designed?” to “how does observation construct what can be designed?”

Argument 06 · thesis

Central thesis: design as recursive distinction

Design is an operation of distinction: it marks what will count as a problem, a user, a need, a requirement, a possible future.

A sociocybernetic ontology understands design as a recursive and conversational production of:

distinctions relations possibilities observability
  • To design is to decide what becomes visible, actionable, desirable, or excluded — an ontological cut with ethical consequences (Spencer-Brown, 1969; Luhmann, 1998).
  • Design does not merely organize form; it organizes observability — it constitutes the horizon in which problems, needs, and solutions can appear at all.
Argument 07 · diagnosis

From a linear chain to a recursive ecology

LINEAR MODEL

Designer→ Artifact→ User GAME OVER

RECURSIVE ECOLOGY — one full loop

distinction→ observation→ pattern recognition→ representation→ decision→ intervention→ perturbation→ reinterpretation→ refactoring→ revised distinction

The Ontology of Design in a Sociocybernetic Key, §4 (Ortega & Cardiel, 2026)

  • The system changes itself while designing: prototypes, data, stories, and user responses re-enter the process and alter future distinctions.
  • Design configures both Umwelt — what participants can do — and Stimmung — how the situation feels: hospitality or suspicion, dignity or surveillance.
Argument 08 · core contribution

Neither designer nor user pre-exist the system

  • Designer ≠ external agent: a self-organizing, second-order observer — structurally coupled to the system in which design becomes possible, and transformed by the act of designing (von Foerster, 2003).
  • User ≠ pre-given social fact: an internal construction of the design system — a projected figure through which the system describes its own purpose and legitimacy. The empirical user appears only as perturbation the system may translate, amplify, or ignore (von Glasersfeld, 1995; Luhmann, 1998).
  • On platforms the boundary destabilizes: users design (prosumers, hackers, makers); designers are users (of software, templates, infrastructures). Both are distributed nodes of observation in recursive ecologies of communication.
“The user is a function of the system’s self-observation.” Manifesto, §3 · orig. “El usuario es una función de la autoobservación del sistema.”
“There are no essences — only distinctions that emerge in the operation of the system.” Ontology of User & Designer, §4 (Ortega & Cardiel, 2025)
Argument 09 · ontological axis

Between techne and tyche: control and contingency

  • Techne: not mere skill but self-accounting production — a logos that must render reasons; design as an epistemic event (Gadamer, 1993/1996, p. 50).
  • Tyche: the goddess of the contingent — chance, the event that escapes control. No design, however calculated, escapes tyche.
  • Recursivity does not eliminate contingency; contingency is constitutive — not noise awaiting elimination (Hui, 2019).

Design happens in the tension between techne and tyche — and perhaps it is tyche that gives design its depth, its openness, its capacity for emergence.

“In every act of design there is a miniaturized ontology — a constitution of the world that becomes conscious of its own constitution.” Notes on a Cyber-Ontology of Design, entry 02 (Ortega & Cardiel, 2025)
Argument 10 · practice again

Designing in conversation with LLMs: Pask’s machines, at last?

  • We now design while conversing with LLMs — the paper, the manifesto, the conference website, and this very deck emerged partly through such conversations. Our practice is our evidence.
  • Pask distinguished communication (signal transfer) from conversation (negotiated understanding). LLMs make conversation — not command — an everyday design medium.
  • The LLM is neither tool nor author: a conversational partner inside the design system → the designer position becomes distributed.
  • Each prompt is a distinction; each response a perturbation the designer must translate — the recursive loop, at high frequency.
  • Techne meets tyche in every generation: intentional prompting × the irreducible contingency of the response (Hui, 2019).
  • From ELIZA’s simulated dialogue (Watzlawick & Krieg, 1991/1995) to conversation that is no longer a simulacrum: Kraków asked about AI — we answer with an ontology of design for the age of conversational machines.

Conclusion · continue?

Changing the question of design

Instead of asking only — What should the designer create for the user?

…a sociocybernetic ontology asks: Through which distinctions does a recursive design system construct designers, users, problems, environments, atmospheres, and possible futures?

Design begins with distinction. Its responsibility is to keep those distinctions observable, contestable, and revisable.

Thank you — this talk, too, is an invitation to conversation.
