Roadmap · one valley, four levels

If the technical becomes visible only when it breaks down, how can critique address infrastructures that escape attention precisely because they work?

The uncanny valley is the privileged site where that question becomes empirical in AI.

01Invisibilization — how technologies disappear into the background (Nosthoff & Maschewski; Latour)
02Interruption — the uncanny valley as spontaneous breakdown (Mori)
03Steering — explainability & alignment as governmentality (Ashby; Nassehi; Foucault)
04Critique — resonance & estrangement as practices for the remainder (Rosa; Plessner; Brecht)

invisibilization · interruption · steering · critique

Argument 01 · invisibilization

Technology disappears into the lifeworld

  • Contemporary technologies become invisible as they become embedded in everyday life — they function as the unnoticed background of experience.
  • Wearables sink into the Erfahrungs- und Lebenswelt, expanding “fast natürlich” — almost naturally — and precisely thereby gain normative Kraft (Nosthoff & Maschewski, 2019, pp. 46–47).
  • Invisibility is not neutrality: the less we see it, the more it formats conduct.
→ → →

Each generation aims at deeper embedding — at disappearing better.

Argument 02 · invisibilization

Visible only in breakdown: the critique dilemma

  • Technical work is blackboxed by its own success: when a machine runs, no one looks inside. The technical becomes visible only when it stops working (Latour, 1999).
  • Technology as the social made durable — the crystallization of social relations into stable, unquestioned forms.
  • The dilemma: critique needs visibility, but well-functioning infrastructure withdraws from view.

Waiting for breakdown is not a method. Is there a breakdown that happens without the system failing?

the black box — cracking
(transition: enter the valley)

Argument 03 · interruption

Enter the valley (Mori, 1970/2012)

  • Affinity rises with human likeness, then plunges into eeriness just before full human presence — “from empathy to revulsion” (Mori, 1970/2012, p. 98).
  • Mori’s own advice: avoid the valley — aim at moderate human likeness. The valley has always been treated as a design flaw to be engineered away (p. 100).
  • Our reframe: the valley is not a defect but a sociotechnical interruption — the moment where assimilation visibly fails.
Argument 04 · interruption

The valley today: AI that fails to disappear

  • LLMs, synthetic voices, generative avatars hover between the familiar and the strange — neither fully naturalized nor fully alien.
  • The unease is informative: it exposes the work required to normalize artificial agents — work that is invisible everywhere else.
  • The valley is a spontaneous breakdown: it returns visibility to the technical without a deliberate critical operation and without the system failing — the answer to the dilemma of Argument 02.

Argument 05 · steering

Change in order not to change

Why does the industry work so hard to smooth the valley? Because systems preserve themselves through regulated change.

disturbance→ regulator→ essential variables kept stable→ adaptation ↻
  • Ashby: regulation blocks the passage of disturbances to the essential variables; the homeostat; ultrastability — stability maintained through adaptation (Ashby, 1956, pp. 42–48, 125–129).
  • Nassehi: Trägheit — social systems persist in their patterns, absorbing irritations without altering structure (Nassehi, 2019).
  • Self-organization is a double figure: inertia (reproducing form) and emergence (producing novelty) — never one without the other.

Smoothing the valley is the paradigm case: the system changes — better avatars, finer voices — precisely in order not to change: to complete invisibilization.

Argument 06 · steering

Steering the drift: governmentality

  • Foucault: to govern is not to prohibit change but to manage circulation — security works on milieus, populations, flows: “the coexistence and circulation of men” (Foucault, 2004/2007, p. 258).
  • Etymology as argument: kybernetes = helmsman — cybernetics and government share a root. To govern is to pilot drift, not to stop the ship.
  • Once AI became socially visible — post-valley — two steering programs emerged to govern it.
opacity → explainability drift → alignment
Argument 07 · steering

Explainability: governing opacity

  • XAI promises to render opaque operations intelligible — but what it produces are acceptable accounts, not windows into the machine.
  • A mode of governmentality: it manages opacity by translating system operations into narratives a social audience can accept (cf. Nassehi, 2019).
  • Constructivist point: every explanation is an observer’s selection — it makes some operations legible while leaving others obscure.

Legibility is redistributed, not achieved.

the lens illuminates one region —
and casts shadow on another

Argument 08 · steering

Alignment: governing drift

  • Alignment promises behavior steered toward acceptable norms — RLHF, constitutions, guardrails.
  • The second mode of governmentality: it manages behavioral drift by translating open-ended behavior into steerable conduct.
  • Alignment is not equilibrium: it is the ongoing steering of structural drift — Ashby’s helmsman, now inside the model. Misalignment is not an accident to be eliminated but the permanent horizon of the practice.

Both programs promise social acceptability: explainability for what the system is, alignment for what it does.

Argument 09 · critique

The remainder: alterity is redistributed, not eliminated

  • Neither explainability nor alignment eliminates the radical otherness of AI models. Explainability leaves obscurity behind every account; alignment modulates the gap without abolishing misalignment.
  • The valley persists — and that persistence is valuable: it is the last place where AI remains visible as technical.
“…a hologram [is] an image of an object which catches not only its actual state but also its interference pattern with other possibilities that were lost when the final state is actualized.” Žižek (2025, p. 29) — the valley is that interference pattern: the lost possibilities of assimilation, still showing

what steering cannot smooth

Argument 10 · critique

Inhabiting the gap I: resonance

  • If the gap cannot be eliminated, how do we live with it? First practice: resonance.
  • Rosa’s Anverwandlung — transformative assimilation: a reciprocal relation in which both sides are changed; the gap is modulated like a musical relation, not abolished (Rosa, 2016/2019).
  • Resonance here is not a feeling but a critical practice: working with the gap as a relation to be tuned.

The warning built into the concept: total resonance would be total invisibilization — perfect assimilation is the end of the relation, not its fulfillment.

Argument 11 · critique

Inhabiting the gap II: estrangement

  • Plessner: eccentric positionality — the human can step outside its own center, place a distance between itself and its experience: “zugleich aus ihr heraus, exzentrisch” (Plessner, 1928/1975, pp. 14–15). Distance is anthropologically ours to take.
  • Brecht: Verfremdungseffekt — make the familiar strange so it becomes visible and thinkable again (Brecht, 1964).
  • Applied to AI: resist premature naturalization — keep the assistant strange, keep the voice marked as synthetic, keep the seams visible. Estrangement does by art what the valley does spontaneously.
“Science fiction is an anti-inevitabilist literature… Far more important than what the gadget does is who it does it to and who it does it for.” Doctorow (2026, pp. 12–13) — estrangement’s political edge: nothing about these systems is inevitable
Conclusiones · continue?

Four takeaways

01 · interruptionThe uncanny valley is a sociotechnical interruption, not a design flaw — sociocybernetics’ privileged empirical site for AI.
02 · governmentalityExplainability and alignment are programs of sociotechnical governmentality — complementary steerings of opacity and drift.
03 · the remainderNeither eliminates alterity. Smoothing the valley completely would complete invisibilization — AI beyond critique.
04 · critiqueThe task shifts from devices of control to a politics of resonance and estrangement: inhabiting the gap, not closing it.
Preserving the valley is preserving the possibility of critique.
